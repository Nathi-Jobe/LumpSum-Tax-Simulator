Why is it not a flat percentage?

Lump sums are taxed in bands. The first part is tax-free, and each higher part is taxed at a higher rate (18%, then 27%, then 36%). A bigger lump sum therefore has a higher overall percentage.

What are the three pots in a two-pot fund?
  • Vested pot: what you had saved up to 31 August 2024, plus its growth. The old rules still apply: cash on leaving your job, or up to one third in cash at retirement.
  • Savings pot: one third of new contributions since 1 September 2024. You can withdraw from it once per tax year (at least R2,000) and it is taxed at your normal tax rate. At retirement the balance can be taken as cash.
  • Retirement pot: two thirds of new contributions. It is locked until retirement and must buy an annuity (unless your fund is very small).
Why does the simulator ask about earlier lump sums?

SARS counts your lump sums over your whole lifetime, not one at a time. Earlier payouts have already used up the cheaper bands, so a new one can be taxed at higher rates.

Retiring or leaving your job? Know your options

Your fund and your severance pay are two different things. Decide on each separately.

At retirement

Cash plus annuity

You can usually take up to one third of the vested pot, plus your savings pot, as cash. The rest buys an annuity that pays you a monthly income. You may take less cash if you prefer more income.

Leaving your job

Take cash now

You can cash out your vested pot (taxed at 18% to 36%, only the first R27,500 is tax-free) and your savings pot (taxed at your normal rate). The retirement pot stays locked.

Leaving your job

Preserve your fund

Move your fund to a preservation fund or leave it where it is. No tax is paid now and it keeps growing. At retirement you get the better tax table, where the first R550,000 is tax-free.

If you are retrenched, the severance package from your employer is separate from your fund and is taxed on the better table too. You can add it in the simulator. Leave pay, notice pay and bonuses are taxed as normal salary and are not included.

Your tax-free amount

Everyone gets a lifetime tax-free allowance on lump sums. Using it wisely can save a lot of tax.

R550,000 for lump sums taken at retirement, and for severance packages, counted over your whole lifetime.

R27,500 for vested cash taken when you leave your job, counted over your lifetime.

No tax-free amount for savings pot withdrawals. They are added to your income and taxed at your normal rate.

Cashing out when you leave your job can use up part of the R550,000 you may prefer to keep for retirement. Speak to an adviser before you cash out.

Method

SARS tables for the 2026/27 tax year (1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027). The lump sum tables have not changed since 1 March 2023.

What each choice does in the simulator
  • Retire: maximum cash is the savings pot plus one third of the vested pot. The retirement pot and the rest of the vested pot buy an annuity. If your retirement pot plus two thirds of your vested pot is R165,000 or less, you may take everything as cash.
  • Leave and take cash: you can reach your vested and savings pots. The retirement pot stays locked. A smaller amount is taken from the vested pot first, then the savings pot. A severance package is assumed to be received first.
  • Leave and preserve: no tax today. The simulator shows what you could take if you retired later, using today's balances and ignoring growth.
Retirement and severance table
  • R0 to R550,000: 0%
  • R550,001 to R770,000: 18% of the amount above R550,000
  • R770,001 to R1,155,000: R39,600 plus 27% above R770,000
  • Above R1,155,000: R143,550 plus 36% above R1,155,000
Vested cash when leaving your job (withdrawal table)
  • R0 to R27,500: 0%
  • R27,501 to R726,000: 18% of the amount above R27,500
  • R726,001 to R1,089,000: R125,730 plus 27% above R726,000
  • Above R1,089,000: R223,740 plus 36% above R1,089,000
Savings pot withdrawals (income tax table)

Taxed as extra income. The simulator works out the tax on your salary with and without the withdrawal, using the 2026/27 brackets (18%, 26%, 31%, 36%, 39%, 41%, 45%) and the R17,820 primary rebate. Age rebates and other deductions are ignored.

How the lump sum tax is worked out

Your earlier lump sums are added to the new one and the table is applied to the total. The tax on the earlier lump sums alone is then subtracted. The difference is the tax on this lump sum. This is the cumulative method SARS uses.

What this simulator does not cover
  • Leave pay, notice pay and bonuses (taxed as salary).
  • The tax on monthly annuity income.
  • Fund-specific rules, tax directives, age rebates and your other income.
